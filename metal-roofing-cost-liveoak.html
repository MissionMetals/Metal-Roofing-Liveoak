Start With What You're Roofing

In Live Oak, the first question is often whether the job is a house, a barn or both. A pole barn with open purlins and a simple slope is a very different job from a two-story farmhouse with porches and dormers. Many Suwannee County homeowners roof the house and a shop at the same time so the color matches and the installer only mobilizes once.

Profile Choice

PBR is the workhorse for agricultural and shop buildings and an economical choice on homes. 5V crimp suits older farmhouses and gives a classic look. Standing seam has concealed fasteners and costs more for both materials and labor. Each one has different trim needs too.

Gauge, Coating and Color

Heavier steel adds cost but stands up better to falling pine limbs. Painted finishes cost more than bare Galvalume, and some colors are priced differently. Make sure each bid you collect names the gauge and finish.

Structure Underneath

A home re-roof may involve tearing off one or two layers of shingles and repairing decking. A barn might need new purlins or added bracing if the old framing is worn. Those structural items can move the total more than the panels themselves, so ask the installer to separate them on the quote.

Distance and Access

Long dirt drives, soft ground after rain and gates that a delivery truck can't fit through all add time. Rural properties out toward McAlpin, O'Brien or Dowling Park may need materials staged differently than a lot in town. Being close to our manufacturing in north Florida helps with delivery, but site access is still part of the price.

Permits

Inside the city, permits go through the City of Live Oak; elsewhere, Suwannee County. Agricultural buildings sometimes have different rules. Your installer confirms what applies.
