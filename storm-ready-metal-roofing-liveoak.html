Inland Doesn't Mean Out of the Path

Live Oak sits about an hour from the Gulf, and plenty of homeowners assume storms lose their punch by the time they get here. Recent Big Bend landfalls have proved otherwise: hurricanes coming ashore near Steinhatchee and Perry have carried damaging wind straight up through Suwannee County, toppling pines onto homes from Wellborn to Branford and leaving rural roads blocked for days.

Add in the everyday summer pattern, afternoon thunderstorms with gusty outflow winds and heavy rain, and Live Oak roofs take a lot of weather.

Open Farmland and Pine Stands

Much of the county is open pasture, hay fields and planted pine. Homes on open land catch more direct wind, while homes surrounded by pines face the risk of trees and limbs coming down. Both affect what the installer specifies: open sites may need tighter fastener spacing at the eaves and corners, and wooded lots benefit from a heavier gauge panel.

How a Metal Roof Holds Together

Metal panels interlock along their length and are screwed into the roof deck or purlins on a set pattern. Long panels run from ridge to eave without the hundreds of individual pieces a shingle roof uses, so there are fewer edges for gusts to peel back. Ridge cap, gable trim and eave trim close off the system so wind can't get underneath.

Barns, Shops and Outbuildings

Suwannee County properties often have a pole barn, equipment shed or poultry house alongside the home. PBR panels are the common choice on these, and they need to be fastened properly to the purlins, too. A detached building with a loose roof can become flying debris that damages the house in a storm.

Before You Call an Installer

  • Note where trees overhang the roof and whether any are leaning.
  • Check if your property is inside Live Oak city limits or unincorporated Suwannee County, since that decides who issues the permit.
  • List every building you want roofed, so the installer can look at them in one visit.
  • Ask the installer how they'll fasten edges on open, windy sites.
