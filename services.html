Matching the Profile to the Building

The farmhouse or family home: 5V crimp and standing seam are the usual picks for a primary residence in Live Oak, whether it's a block home in town or a house set back on acreage toward Wellborn or McAlpin.

Hay barns, pole barns and equipment sheds: PBR is a workhorse for open-sided and enclosed ag buildings. Its wider ribs span purlins well and cover large roof areas efficiently.

Shops, carports and lean-tos: Many Suwannee County properties add a workshop or a lean-to on the side of a barn. Using the same color and trim keeps everything on the property looking consistent.

Re-covering an older metal roof: If an existing barn or outbuilding already has worn metal, ask your installer whether it should be replaced or re-covered. They'll check the framing and fastening first.

What to Ask Your Installer

Before you sign anything, it's worth asking the licensed installer a few questions: Which metal roofing profile do they recommend for each building and why? What underlayment and fastener pattern will meet code at your site? Who pulls the permit with the City of Live Oak or Suwannee County? How will they handle ridge vents, skylights or pipe boots? Get the answers in writing as part of their quote.

Choosing Colors for Rural North Florida Properties

White, light gray and tan are popular for keeping homes and barns cooler under the summer sun. Galvalume and earth tones blend well with pasture and pine settings, and charcoal or black are common on newer farmhouse builds. Your installer can bring samples so you can see them in person.
